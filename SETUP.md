# Setup: the interview

Answer these questions in plain language. Your answers become the first draft of your core files. Short answers are fine. You can revise everything later; the system is built to be corrected.

## About you (goes to core/USER.md)

1. What is your name, and what should the assistant call you?
2. Where are you based, and what timezone?
3. What do you do, in two or three sentences?
4. What are the three things you are working on that matter most right now?
5. What should the assistant never assume about you?

## How the assistant should work (goes to core/AGENTS.md)

6. When you give an instruction, how literal should the assistant be?
7. What must it always check before reporting something as done?
8. What must it never do without asking you first?
9. How do you want mistakes handled?

## Voice and persona (goes to core/SOUL.md)

10. How should the assistant sound: direct, warm, formal, playful?
11. Words or phrases it should never use when writing for you?
12. Three adjectives for the writing voice you want in your own content?

## Memory habits (goes to core/MEMORY.md)

13. What kinds of facts are worth keeping permanently?
14. What should be forgotten quickly, or never stored?
15. Is there anything sensitive that needs special handling rules?

## Your world

16. Who are the five people the assistant will hear about most? (Names and relationships only. You will give each a page later.)
17. What groups, communities, or teams are you part of?
18. What will you look up more than once? (Those become your first registers.)
19. Which optional modules fit your work: workflow gates, research evidence standards, writing voice, business operations, something else?

## After the interview

- Draft the five core files from your answers.
- Create one page per person and group named above.
- Create one register per repeat lookup.
- Pin your rules as version 1.0 (a git tag works) so future changes are deliberate.
